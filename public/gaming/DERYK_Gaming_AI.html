01 / Overview

Game worlds need agents that understand them.

Scripted behavior breaks the moment a player does something unexpected. Players notice when enemies ignore cover, allies block doorways, or a shopkeeper forgets what happened a minute ago.

DERYK connects what an agent can see, what it remembers, and what it can do into a single world model, so characters respond to the game as it actually unfolds.

02 / Architecture

A layered approach to game intelligence

Separating world understanding from action planning keeps behavior predictable, debuggable, and tunable by designers.

Layer 01

World model

Builds a live map of terrain, objects, players, and events, with memory of what changed and where.

Layer 02

Decision and action

Chooses goals, scores candidate moves, and sends inputs to the character through the same controls a player would use.

03 / Capabilities

Foundational capabilities

Connected capabilities that turn raw game state into believable, skilled play.

Scene context

Level Understanding

Reads geometry, cover, navigation space, and line of sight in any level, including ones it has never seen.

Entity continuity

Player and Entity Tracking

Follows players, allies, and enemies through occlusion and respawns, keeping a consistent identity for each.

Persistent memory

Persistent World State

Remembers past encounters, player habits, and changes to the world across a match or an entire campaign.

Predictive planning

Tactics and Prediction

Anticipates where players will move and what they will try next, then plans flanks, retreats, and timing.

06 / Safety

Built for fair, controllable play

Designers and players stay in control of what agents are allowed to know and do.

Same information as players

Agents only see what a player in their position could see, with no hidden map knowledge unless a designer enables it.

Tunable difficulty

Reaction time, aim error, and aggression are set by designers and held within clear limits at every skill level.

Auditable decisions

Every action is logged with the reasons behind it, so teams can replay and debug unexpected behavior.

Content guardrails

Generated dialogue is filtered against your rating and tone rules before it reaches the player.
